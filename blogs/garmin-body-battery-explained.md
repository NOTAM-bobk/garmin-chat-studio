Garmin Body Battery is an estimate of your body's available energy reserves. It can be a useful way to notice how sleep, stress and activity line up over time, but it is not a literal fuel gauge and cannot tell you by itself whether you are ready to train.

## What goes into the estimate

Garmin describes Body Battery as using heart-rate variability, stress, sleep quality and activity data. The score ranges from 5 to 100 on supported devices. Garmin Connect may also show **Charged** and **Drained** values: estimated recovery and energy use over a period. Availability and details vary by device.

That matters because the number is an algorithmic summary, not a lab measurement. Two people with the same score can feel very different, and your own pattern is more useful than a comparison with someone else's.

## Why it may not recharge overnight

A long night does not guarantee a high morning score. Garmin says stress levels and overall recovery also influence Body Battery. A late hard session, illness, travel or a stressful day may coincide with less overnight recharge, but the score alone cannot identify which factor caused it.

If a low reading surprises you, check the surrounding context rather than reaching for one explanation:

- Look at several days of Body Battery, not one morning.
- Compare sleep and overnight stress with your normal pattern.
- Check recent activity and other available recovery signals.
- Note whether the watch was worn consistently and synced.

## Use the trend, not a pass/fail cutoff

Treat Body Battery as a prompt to pay attention, not a training command. A low estimate alongside poor sleep and a run of harder-than-usual sessions may support choosing an easier day. A single low value with otherwise normal readings may not justify changing a plan.

Likewise, a high score is not proof that an intense session is safe. Consider your training schedule, perceived effort and any symptoms. Wearable data is not a substitute for medical care.

## Keep the limitations in view

Body Battery is available only on compatible Garmin products, and its inputs can differ by device. Missing wear time, sensor fit and sync gaps can limit the picture. Do not interpret an absent reading as zero or assume two device models calculate every displayed metric identically.

The practical question is not "Is my score good?" It is "What changed in my own pattern, and does another signal support that explanation?"
