Heart-rate zones turn a stream of beats per minute into broad effort bands. Garmin uses your profile settings to assign zones, but the default settings are only a starting point: wrong inputs can make a workout appear easier or harder than it was.

## What the zones describe

Garmin describes five commonly used zones, from easy effort in Zone 1 to very hard effort in Zone 5. The ranges are expressed in beats per minute and, on supported devices, may be customized. Garmin notes that zones are typically based on maximum heart rate by default; settings and options differ across products.

The zone label is not the effort itself. Heat, hills, fatigue, medication and sensor quality can change the relationship between heart rate and pace or power. Talk-test and perceived effort remain useful checks, especially when the reading seems surprising.

## Check the settings before interpreting a chart

If time-in-zone looks implausible, review the inputs before changing the workout:

- Check whether your recorded maximum heart rate is current and plausible.
- Review resting heart rate if your zone method uses it.
- Look for activity-specific zone settings on your device or in Garmin Connect.
- Confirm the activity used a reliable heart-rate source and synced correctly.

A zone boundary based on a stale maximum heart rate can shift every band. Updating the profile may also affect how later activities are classified; check Garmin's current guidance for how changes apply to previously recorded activities.

## Use zone time for the question it can answer

Zone distribution can help describe how a session was recorded: for example, how much time the watch classified in easier or harder bands. It does not by itself prove that a session met its intended purpose. Pair it with duration, pace or power where available, terrain, and how the effort felt.

When comparing two runs, compare like with like: similar sport, route or conditions, duration, and zone settings. If settings changed between activities, say so and avoid treating the zone totals as directly equivalent.

## Zones are a guide, not a diagnosis

Heart rate is one signal among several. A single unusually high or low reading may reflect sensor error, conditions or an individual response. If a reading is accompanied by concerning symptoms, do not use a training zone chart to assess your health; seek appropriate medical advice.
